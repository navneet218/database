# Personal branding outreach playbook

How personal branding agencies in the GLIPED space get senior leaders onto discovery calls, and a step-by-step system to find, qualify and personalise outreach for founders, CXOs and senior leaders in India, the US, the UK and Canada.

---

## 1. How agencies like GLIPED actually win clients

Most personal branding agencies that sell to founders and executives run a mix of the same six motions. The ones with good close rates lean on the first three.

| Motion | What it looks like | Why it works |
|---|---|---|
| Agency founder as proof | The agency founder posts daily on LinkedIn about ghostwriting, founder brands, inbound deals. Prospects follow them for months before booking. | The buyer is paying for LinkedIn skill. The founder's own profile is the portfolio. |
| Free audit as the opener | "I looked at your profile and last 10 posts, here are 3 things costing you reach." Delivered as a Loom, a PDF teardown or a short DM. | It gives value before asking, and it shows the gap in the prospect's own words and numbers. |
| Warm-up before the DM | 1–2 weeks of thoughtful comments on the prospect's posts before connecting. | The prospect recognises the name when the DM arrives. Acceptance and reply rates go up a lot. |
| Referral and partner channels | VCs, PR firms, executive coaches, EAs/chiefs of staff, event organisers who pass on their portfolio founders. | One partner can send several warm leads a quarter. |
| Case-study-led outbound | "We took [similar founder] from 2k to 25k followers and 4 inbound deals in 6 months." | Senior buyers want a peer who looks like them, not a generic result. |
| Lead magnets and events | Profile checklists, "founder content" webinars, LinkedIn Lives, small roundtables. | Builds a list of people who have raised their hand. |

What the better agencies avoid: templated "I help CEOs build their personal brand" pitches, pitching in the connection note, and chasing people who have no reason to care about LinkedIn right now.

## 2. Who to target (ICP)

### Core roles
- Founders and co-founders (seed to Series C, or bootstrapped at meaningful revenue)
- CEOs, MDs, country heads
- CXOs: CMO, CTO, CFO, CHRO, CRO, COO
- VPs and SVPs in companies of 200+ people who are building a public profile for their next move
- Partners at consulting, law, VC and PE firms (their pipeline depends on reputation)

### Best-fit company types
- B2B SaaS, fintech, AI, D2C, professional services, VC/PE, healthcare, edtech
- Companies where the founder's visibility drives sales, hiring or fundraising

### Regional notes
| Region | Sweet spot | Tone that works | Watch out for |
|---|---|---|---|
| India | Funded startup founders, D2C founders, CXOs at large Indian corporates and GCCs, family-business second generation | Warm, respectful, relationship first. Referencing a mutual connection or shared event helps a lot. | Price sensitivity at early stage. Many decisions go through a CoS/EA. Move to WhatsApp only after they share their number. |
| US | Series A–C founders, VC partners, B2B SaaS CEOs, consultants/coaches with a high ticket | Direct, short, outcome-focused. Numbers beat adjectives. | Very high DM volume, so generic messages get ignored. Expect to compete with US agencies on proof. |
| UK | Founders in London/Manchester tech, partners in professional services, fintech leaders | Understated and plain. Low hype. A dry, honest line lands better than big claims. | Hard-sell language backfires. UK GDPR/PECR rules apply to email. |
| Canada | Toronto/Vancouver/Montreal tech founders, fintech and AI leaders, professional services | Polite, clear, practical. Similar to US but a little less aggressive. | CASL is strict on commercial email. Get consent or rely on a clear business relevance exemption. Montreal: consider French. |

## 3. Signals: who is actually in-market

Score each prospect across four signal types. The best leads show at least one strong intent signal plus one trigger.

### A. Direct intent signals (strongest)
- Posted or commented asking for a ghostwriter, LinkedIn agency, content strategist or "personal branding help"
- Engaged (liked/commented) with posts from personal branding agencies, ghostwriters or LinkedIn growth creators
- Follows LinkedIn creators on founder-led content, or joined groups on LinkedIn growth
- Visited your profile or your company page (check "Who viewed your profile" daily)
- Liked or commented on your own posts
- Downloaded a lead magnet, attended a webinar or LinkedIn Live
- Recently started LinkedIn Premium / Creator mode, or turned on "Follow" as the primary button
- Posts saying "I should post more", "starting to write here", "day 1 of posting"

### B. Trigger events (a reason to care now)
- Just raised a round (Crunchbase, Inc42, Entrackr, YourStory, TechCrunch, Sifted, BetaKit)
- New role in the last 90 days (CXO appointment, promoted to CEO, new country head)
- Company launch, rebrand, new product, expansion into a new country
- Hiring spree, especially senior hires (founder brand helps hiring)
- Speaking at a conference, launching a podcast, writing a book
- Company announced an IPO plan or big partnership
- Founder left a job to start something (needs an audience fast)
- Senior leader "open to" new roles or board positions

### C. Profile gap signals (the pain you can point at)
- 5k+ connections or followers but posts get under 30 reactions
- Posts irregularly: bursts then gaps of weeks
- Only reshares company posts, no original writing
- Weak headline (just a job title), no banner, old photo, empty About section
- Strong offline reputation (press, awards, big company) but a thin LinkedIn presence
- Competitors or peers in the same space post well and get visibly more reach

### D. Fit and ability-to-pay signals
- Company funded or at a revenue level that supports a monthly retainer
- Team size 20+ (or a solo expert with high-ticket offers)
- Active on LinkedIn in the last 30 days (logged in, commented, posted). Inactive profiles rarely convert.

### Simple scoring model (use in your CRM)

| Signal | Points |
|---|---|
| Direct intent signal (A) | +30 each, cap 45 |
| Trigger event in last 90 days (B) | +20 each, cap 30 |
| Clear profile gap (C) | +10 each, cap 20 |
| Fit: funded/revenue, team 20+ | +10 |
| Active on LinkedIn in last 30 days | +10 |
| Mutual connection or shared community | +10 |
| Not posted or logged in for 90+ days | −20 |
| Already works with an agency/ghostwriter (visible) | −15 |

- 70+: hot. Personal Loom audit plus multi-channel sequence.
- 40–69: warm. Warm-up comments, then connect and DM sequence.
- Under 40: nurture. Add to a content-engagement list, revisit monthly.

## 4. Where and how to find them

### LinkedIn Sales Navigator filters
- Geography: India / United States / United Kingdom / Canada (or specific metros)
- Seniority: Owner/Partner, CXO, VP
- Function or title: Founder, Co-founder, CEO, Managing Director, Chief * Officer
- Company headcount: 11–50, 51–200, 201–500, 501–1000 (pick per offer)
- Spotlights: "Posted on LinkedIn in past 30 days", "Changed jobs in past 90 days", "Following your company", "Viewed your profile recently", "Connections of" your clients
- Company filters: "Growth in headcount", recent funding (via account lists)

Example boolean title search:
```
("founder" OR "co-founder" OR "CEO" OR "managing director" OR "chief marketing officer" OR "CMO" OR "CTO" OR "CRO") NOT ("assistant" OR "intern" OR "student" OR "freelance")
```

Example LinkedIn post search (for intent):
```
"looking for a ghostwriter" OR "recommend a LinkedIn agency" OR "help with personal branding" OR "who writes your LinkedIn posts"
```
Sort by "Latest" and check weekly.

### Other sources
- Engagers on competitor agency posts and on big personal-branding creators (scrape or review manually)
- Funding news: Inc42, Entrackr, YourStory (India); Crunchbase, TechCrunch (US); Sifted, UKTN (UK); BetaKit (Canada)
- Speaker lists for conferences (SaaSBOOMi, Nasscom, Web Summit Vancouver, Collision/Toronto Tech Week, SaaStr, London Tech Week)
- Podcast guest lists in your niches
- Your own profile viewers and post engagers (the warmest list you have)

## 6. The outreach sequence (channel by channel)

A 21-day, multi-channel sequence for warm and hot leads. Hot leads get the Loom audit on day 7.

| Day | Channel | Action |
|---|---|---|
| 1–6 | LinkedIn | Follow them. Leave 2–3 real comments on their posts (add a point, a question or a relevant stat; never "Great post!"). |
| 3 | LinkedIn | View their profile (they'll see you if you're on Premium). |
| 7 | LinkedIn | Connection request. No pitch. Short note tied to their content or trigger. |
| 8–9 | LinkedIn | On accept: a thank-you message with one observation or question. No link, no pitch. |
| 10–11 | LinkedIn | Value message: offer the audit or share a relevant insight. Ask permission ("Want me to send it over?"). |
| 12 | Loom / PDF | For hot leads or those who said yes: a 2–3 minute Loom going through their profile and last few posts, with 3 fixes. |
| 14 | Email | If you have a verified work email: short email referencing the LinkedIn conversation, with the case study most like them. |
| 16 | LinkedIn | Voice note (30–45 sec). Personal, calm, one question. Works especially well in India and with founders. |
| 19 | LinkedIn | Soft call ask with a specific time window and a reason ("15 minutes to show you the content plan we'd run for you"). |
| 21 | LinkedIn | Breakup message. Leave the door open and keep engaging with their content. |

After day 21, move non-responders to a monthly nurture: keep commenting, and re-engage when a new trigger appears.

### Rules that protect reply rates and the account
- Keep LinkedIn connection requests around 15–25 a day and 100 a week or lower, depending on account age and SSI.
- Keep messages under 80 words. Senior people read on phones.
- One question per message.
- Send in their time zone: Tuesday–Thursday, 8–10am or 5–7pm local.
  - India IST, UK GMT/BST, US ET/PT, Canada ET/PT.
- Never use their company logo, fake familiarity, or claim a mutual connection that doesn't exist.

## 7. Message templates (fill the brackets from the research card)

### Connection notes (under 200 characters)

Trigger based:
> Hi [Name], saw the [Series A / new role] news, congrats. I read your post on [topic] and liked the point about [detail]. Would be good to connect.

Content based:
> Hi [Name], your post on [topic] stuck with me, especially [specific line]. I write about founder content and would like to follow along.

Mutual based:
> Hi [Name], [mutual] and I were talking about founder-led content and your name came up. Would be glad to connect.

### After they accept
> Thanks for connecting, [Name]. Quick question: is LinkedIn something you're putting real time into this year, or more of a side channel for now?

(This qualifies them. "Real time" leads to the audit offer. "Side channel" leads to a nurture or a "what would change that?" reply.)

### Audit offer
> I went through your last 10 posts. Your [format/topic] posts get about [X]x more engagement than the rest, and the [headline/About] isn't pointing people anywhere yet. I put 3 changes together that I think would help. Want me to send a short video?

### Persona angles

Founder raising or just raised:
> Investors and senior hires check your LinkedIn before they reply. Right now your profile says [observation]. We help founders like [similar client] turn that into [result].

CXO in a new role:
> The first 6 months in a new role is when people decide what you stand for. A few posts a month on [their expertise] would do a lot for how the market sees [Company] under you.

Partner at a firm / consultant:
> Most of your peers' pipeline comes from referrals. The ones posting regularly on [topic] are also getting inbound from people they've never met.

Senior leader eyeing the next move (be careful and indirect):
> The leaders who get board and advisory invites usually have a visible point of view. You clearly have one on [topic]; it's just not on LinkedIn yet.

### Regional tweaks
- India: open with warmth and context ("Hope the new quarter is going well"). Mention shared cities, institutes (IIT/IIM/ISB) or events if real. Offer a call time in IST, and flexibility for later evening slots.
- US: skip the small talk. Lead with the number or observation. "Worth a 15-min call Thursday?"
- UK: cut the superlatives. "Might be useful" and "no pressure either way" work better than "huge opportunity".
- Canada: polite and practical. Include a clear opt-out line in any email. French for Quebec-based leads where they post in French.

### Call ask
> Would a 20-minute call next [day] work? I'll bring a draft 30-day content plan for you specifically, and you can decide from there if it's worth doing together.

### Breakup
> I'll stop filling your inbox, [Name]. If LinkedIn moves up the list later this year, I'm easy to find. Either way, I'll keep reading your posts on [topic].

### Cold email (second channel)

Every lead also gets a customised cold email, used when the LinkedIn connection isn't accepted within 5 days or the first DM gets no reply. Rules are in `.claude/skills/gliped-lead-batch/SKILL.md`, and all current emails are in `batches/cold-emails.md`.

- Subject: 2 to 5 words, specific to them.
- Body: 40 to 90 words. Trigger hook, the gap, one line on Gliped, then one question as the CTA.
- Sign-off with an opt-out line. Verified work email only. One follow-up after 5 days.

## 8. Running the discovery call so it leads to a close

Before the call (15 min):
- Update the research card. Check whether they posted since your last message.
- Prepare 2 example posts written in their voice on their topics. This does more than any deck.
- Pick the one case study closest to their role, stage and region.

Call structure (30 minutes):
1. Context (3 min): why they took the call, what's changed.
2. Goals (8 min): what LinkedIn should do for them in 6–12 months. Hiring, fundraising, sales, speaking, next role. Get a number where possible.
3. Current state (5 min): what they've tried, why it stalled (usually time and consistency).
4. Show, don't tell (8 min): the audit findings and the 2 sample posts.
5. How working together looks (4 min): cadence, their time commitment (for example 30 min a week for an interview), approval flow.
6. Next step (2 min): book the strategy/proposal call on the spot, within 3–5 days.

Questions that qualify well:
- "If this worked, what would be different in 6 months?"
- "Who else needs to be involved in deciding this?" (in India and in larger companies this is often a CMO, CoS or EA)
- "What's stopped you posting consistently so far?"
- "Have you worked with an agency or ghostwriter before? What didn't work?"
- "Is there a budget range you had in mind, or should I show you options?"

After the call:
- Same day: recap message with their goals in their words, plus the time for the next call.
- Second call: the strategy deck (Gap → Fix → Plan → Working Together). The `gliped-strategy-deck` skill builds this from the discovery notes.
- Offer a paid 30-day pilot if they hesitate on a longer commitment.
- Follow up on day 2, 5 and 10 after the proposal if there's silence, each time with something new (a post draft, a stat about their peers, a result from a client).

## 9. Metrics to track weekly

| Stage | Metric | Rough target to aim for |
|---|---|---|
| Sourcing | Leads scored 40+ per week | 100–150 |
| Connect | Acceptance rate | 35–50% with warm-up, 20–30% cold |
| Reply | Reply rate on first DM after accept | 20–35% |
| Audit | Audit offer accepted | 30–50% of repliers |
| Booking | Discovery calls booked / connected leads | 5–10% |
| Show-up | Calls held / booked | 75–85% (send a reminder with the agenda) |
| Close | Won / held discovery calls | 20–30% |

These are rough starting targets based on how warm, personalised LinkedIn outreach usually performs for high-ticket services. Replace them with your own numbers after 4–6 weeks.

Track by region, persona and signal type. Within a month you'll see which signals (for example "just raised" vs "engaged with a competitor") actually turn into clients, and you can shift the weekly lead mix toward them.

## 10. Compliance basics

- LinkedIn: automation tools that scrape or auto-send can get accounts restricted. Keep volumes human and review anything automated.
- India: the Digital Personal Data Protection Act, 2023 applies to personal data you store. Keep only what you need and delete on request. Don't add people to WhatsApp broadcasts without consent.
- US: CAN-SPAM for email. Accurate sender, a physical address and an opt-out in every email.
- UK: UK GDPR and PECR. B2B email to corporate addresses is allowed under legitimate interest if it's relevant to their role, with a clear opt-out. Sole traders and partnerships are treated like individuals and need consent.
- Canada: CASL is the strictest. Commercial email needs express or implied consent. A published business email can count as implied consent only when the message relates to their role. Always include identification and an unsubscribe.

## 11. Weekly operating rhythm

| Day | Task |
|---|---|
| Monday | Pull new leads from Sales Nav, funding news, profile viewers and competitor engagers. Score them. |
| Tue–Thu | Warm-up comments (30 min/day), connection requests, DMs, Looms for hot leads. |
| Daily | Check profile viewers and post engagers. Reply to all messages within a few hours during their working day. |
| Friday | Review metrics, move leads between stages, plan next week's own posts (your content is still the biggest source of trust). |

Post 3–4 times a week on your own profile through all of this. Most of the prospects above will check your profile before replying, and what they see there decides whether they book.
